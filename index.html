About Me

The strategist behind
the brands

I am a Social Media Manager and Brand/Content Strategist with over 3 years of experience building brand presence, content systems, and engaged communities from the ground up. My work sits upstream of posting — it starts with research: who the audience actually is, what the competitive landscape looks like, and what content pillars will earn trust before they earn a sale. Then I build the systems — brand voice, content calendars, community management, and paid media — that carry that thinking into daily execution.

I have developed distinct brand identities and content strategies across fintech & Web3, fashion, food and lifestyle, bridal styling, health education, and legal technology — often managing several entirely different brand voices at the same time. Across TikTok, Instagram, X, LinkedIn, Facebook, and Telegram, I combine creative direction with research and analytics — using real audience data and competitive intelligence to build content that converts, not just content that posts.

My story started almost as a hobby. I was asked to help because I was available and, honestly, because I am chronically online. I'd always noticed the gaps — brands that could be positioned better, messaging that could hit harder, research that was missing before the posting even began. It started with helping my brother get his brand on Instagram, and something clicked. What started as a favour became a career built on understanding brands before building for them.

I am also a Computer Science and Software Engineering graduate — which makes me a different kind of strategist, equally comfortable in a competitive analysis spreadsheet as I am in TikTok Studio. Away from screens, I read fiction obsessively, listen to music every single day without exception, travel whenever I can, and consider the beach my safest place in the world.

Bricolagebytinuke — Crafted Confidence
FashionTikTokFeb–Mar 2026
+
The Strategic Problem

A Nigerian fashion brand needed a TikTok presence from zero — 175 followers, no content history, no proof of what this audience even responds to. The job wasn't "post consistently," it was figure out, through testing, exactly what a style-savvy Nigerian audience wants to see — before scaling spend or effort behind it.

The Approach

Built a multi-pillar content system — trending audio, styling videos, GRWM, behind-the-scenes process content, and a deliberate repurposing schedule — then used each month's analytics to refine the next. By month three, the data pointed clearly to process/craft content as the breakthrough format, so the content calendar was rebuilt around it.

The Result

Comments and shares — the higher-value signals over passive views — grew 209% and 314% respectively across the engagement. A repurposed video outperformed both its originals by +562 views, +54 likes, and +5 saves, proving the repurposing strategy wasn't a guess — it was a system that worked.

03
The Create Company — Bakery + 3 Affiliated Bridal & Styling Brands
Multi-BrandFood & LifestyleBridal & StylingTikTokInstagram2025–2026
+
The Strategic Problem

A Le Cordon Bleu-trained luxury bakery needed a digital presence that matched its product quality. The scope quickly expanded beyond the bakery itself to three affiliated bridal and styling brands under the same umbrella — each with a distinct audience, voice, and visual identity that needed to run independently and simultaneously without diluting one another or the parent brand.

The Multi-Brand Approach

Built and managed four parallel content strategies under one roof: The Create Company (aesthetic bakery content and behind-the-scenes process), @thebridalboudoirco (destination bridal styling across Lagos), @thegroomsboudoir (groom styling and couple styling for destination weddings), and @bbstyleco (occasion and event styling). Each brand had its own content pillars, posting cadence, and voice — including culturally specific bridal moments (Igbo, Igala, Ghanaian) and destination wedding storytelling — all developed and executed at the same time.

The Result

Running four brand identities simultaneously didn't dilute any of them. The Create Company's cultural moment — the IShowSpeed 21st-birthday-cake series — became its defining content arc at 71.9K views on the lead video. In parallel, @thebridalboudoirco grew to 182.8K TikTok likes. This is the clearest proof point of the ability to hold multiple brand voices distinctly and run them all at a high standard, at once.

Multi-BrandInstagramFacebookMeta Ads2023–2025

The Strategic Problem

Four genuinely different businesses — hair, beddings, electrical retail, and a personal brand — needed to grow simultaneously, each with its own audience and product category. The risk wasn't running out of content ideas. It was letting four brand voices blur into one generic feed.

The Approach

Built fully independent strategies per brand — separate content calendars, separate Meta Ads targeting, and separate WhatsApp/Instagram/Facebook community management — so each audience experienced a brand that felt built specifically for them, not a shared template.

Frans Hair grew from under 2,000 to 8,259 verified Instagram followers — over 300% growth — while the personal brand reached 5.7K Facebook followers. All four brands stayed active and converting for two consistent years, proving the multi-brand system held up over time, not just a single campaign cycle.

05
TOMA Legal Tech — Go-To-Market & Campaign Strategy
Legal TechMarket ResearchCampaign PlanningNigeria · Ghana · KenyaJune 2026
+
The Strategic Problem

TOMA Legal Tech needed go-to-market strategies for two products — Retrieve, a legal research platform, and Certify, a compliance certification platform — on a shared, limited budget. The real challenge wasn't picking channels. It was identifying the single highest-urgency audience in each market and proving conversion before spreading budget thin across markets, products, and platforms.

The Approach

Ran competitive analysis across 6 competitors (LawPavilion, JUDY, Legalpedia, ACAMS, and others), segmented audiences by urgency rather than size — final-year law students and sole practitioners for Retrieve, compliance officers facing a 2026 regulatory deadline for Certify — and built phased rollouts that proved conversion in Nigeria before expanding to Ghana and Kenya. Every channel recommendation was tied to a specific audience trigger, not a platform trend.

The Result

Delivered two full campaign strategies with budget allocation logic, country-specific messaging, competitor positioning gaps, and measurement frameworks built around one north-star metric per product: paid subscribers and paid enrolments per spend — not views, not followers. Proof that the strategic thinking behind a content calendar works the same whether the product is a fashion brand or a legal research platform.

Elizabeth Adewale — SEO & Content Strategy
SEOContent StrategyHealth Education2024
+
The Strategic Problem

A health education platform covering sexual and reproductive health needed organic visibility — but ranking content in this niche required more than keywords. It needed technical precision and enough content sensitivity to build genuine authority without losing nuance.

The Approach

Ran a full keyword gap analysis, restructured the page hierarchy around it, rewrote meta titles and descriptions, and built an internal linking system feeding a 4-week content roadmap — all tracked through Google Search Console and Analytics from day one.

The Result

PageSpeed Insights scores hit 98/100 mobile and 100/100 desktop — a near-perfect technical foundation. Google Search Console flagged a milestone: 120 organic clicks in a single 28-day period, with average time on page up 35%, evidence the content itself was earning attention, not just rankings.
